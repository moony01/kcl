"I'm so, so proud to be Korean."

On February 1, 2026, at the 68th Grammy Awards ceremony, **EJAE** — one of the vocal artists behind the fictional girl group HUNTR/X from the Netflix film "KPop Demon Hunters" — spoke these words with tears in her eyes during her acceptance speech. It was **the first time in history** that a K-pop artist had lifted a Grammy trophy. The dream that BTS couldn't achieve despite three consecutive nominations was realized first through a movie soundtrack.

## How 'Golden' Conquered the Grammys

'Golden' is a track performed by the fictional K-pop group HUNTR/X from the Netflix film "KPop Demon Hunters." The actual vocals were delivered by three artists: **EJAE, Audrey Nuna, and REI AMI**. The film tells the story of a K-pop group that protects the world from supernatural beings.

Upon its release in summer 2025, the song immediately claimed the **#1 spot on the Billboard Hot 100**, becoming a worldwide mega-hit. The 'Golden Challenge' that started on TikTok surpassed **1 billion views**, with videos of global fans singing along to the Korean lyrics spreading explosively. With David Guetta's remix version adding fuel to the fire, 'Golden' transcended being a mere movie OST to become **the biggest crossover hit of 2025**.

At the 68th Grammys, 'Golden' earned an impressive **4 nominations**:
- **Best Song Written for Visual Media** (Won)
- **Song of the Year**
- **Best Pop Duo/Group Performance**
- **Best Compilation Soundtrack** (album category)

Ultimately taking home the **Best Song Written for Visual Media** award, it created the historic moment of a K-pop artist's first Grammy win.

## The Dream BTS Couldn't Achieve — A Movie OST Opens the Door First

K-pop's relationship with the Grammys carries a long and bittersweet history.

BTS was nominated for **three consecutive years** from 2021 to 2023 in the Best Pop Duo/Group Performance category, but failed to win each time. BLACKPINK's Rosé and Bruno Mars' collaboration 'APT.' achieved the remarkable feat of being nominated for **Song of the Year** at this year's Grammys, but the award went to Billie Eilish.

This is exactly why 'Golden's' win carries such weight. It opened the Grammy door not through pure K-pop music, but through **a fusion with a Hollywood film**. Music critic Hee-yun analyzed the win as "an effect of KPop Demon Hunters' global reach." The combination of K-pop's musical prowess with **Netflix as a global platform** broke down the perception barriers among Grammy voters.

## What EJAE's Tears Tell Us

At the press conference following the win, EJAE shared:

> "Growing up, people didn't know where Korea was or what Korea was. That's why it's so incredible to have the song 'Golden' being sung all over the world, singing the Korean lyrics word by word."

This statement isn't merely personal emotion. It **symbolizes the moment K-pop is recognized as 'music of the world' rather than 'music from Asia.'** The Grammys remain the epicenter of the Western music industry, and a K-pop artist holding a trophy there signifies the collapse of cultural barriers.

Particularly noteworthy is that the winning song 'Golden' **contains Korean lyrics**. A song in a non-English language winning at a major Grammy category is rare in itself, proving that K-pop's **linguistic barrier is no longer a limitation**.

## The Ripple Effects on the K-pop Industry

'Golden's' Grammy win is expected to generate multiple ripple effects across the entire K-pop industry.

**First, the acceleration of Hollywood-K-pop collaborations.** Netflix has already announced a sequel to 'KPop Demon Hunters' (projected for 2029). Hollywood studios witnessing this success will more aggressively pursue collaborations with K-pop artists.

**Second, a shift in Grammy strategy.** Traditional K-pop agencies have challenged the Grammys with pure music, but this win presents a new strategic pathway through **film and drama OSTs**. With HYBE, SM, and JYP all expanding their visual content businesses, this route is highly realistic.

**Third, the elevation of K-pop's cultural status.** A Grammy win means more than a music award. It represents official recognition from the mainstream American music industry, directly impacting K-pop artists' **US tour scale, brand deals, and media exposure**.

## The First Trophy, But Certainly Not the Last

'Golden's' Grammy win isn't the end — it's the **beginning of a new chapter**. BTS's full-group comeback is scheduled for March 2026, and BLACKPINK's Rosé has already been nominated for Song of the Year. The day when K-pop moves from being a 'nomination regular' to a 'winning powerhouse' at the Grammys is not far away.

The message in EJAE's tears is clear. **K-pop is no longer knocking on the door — it has opened the door and walked through.** Which K-pop artist will stand on that stage at the next Grammys?
