"K-pop just arrived at Carnival."

On February 16, 2026, in São Paulo's Ibirapuera Park, a K-pop group stood at the heart of one of the world's largest festivals — **Brazil's Carnival** — for the very first time in history. NMIXX appeared as special guests at Pabllo Vittar's 'Bloco da Pabllo' block party, opening an entirely new chapter in K-pop's global story.

Two million people filling the streets. Korean and Portuguese vocals echoing from atop a Trio Elétrico stage truck. This was no ordinary overseas performance. It was the signal that **JYP Entertainment's Latin America strategy has officially launched**.

## Understanding Carnival — The Weight of 2 Million

To grasp the significance of this moment, you need to understand what Carnival means to Brazil.

Carnival is Brazil's **national identity** in festival form. Every February, millions descend upon Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo for five days of celebration that transcend music, dance, and culture. The 2026 São Paulo Carnival ran from February 14 to 18, and the 'Blocos' — massive street block parties — regularly attract hundreds of thousands to millions of attendees.

Pabllo Vittar is one of Brazil's most influential pop artists. The 'Bloco da Pabllo' is consistently one of Carnival's most talked-about events. Simply being invited to perform is a stamp of approval from Brazilian mainstream culture.

NMIXX standing on that stage declares that K-pop is no longer confined to Asia and North America — it has **entered the mainstream of South American popular culture**.

## "MEXE" — Where Korean Meets Portuguese

NMIXX's Carnival appearance didn't happen overnight. Back in August 2025, NMIXX and Pabllo Vittar released their collaboration single **"MEXE."**

"MEXE" fused NMIXX's signature 'MIXX POP' sound with Brazilian pop styling. The most striking element: **lyrics in both Korean and Portuguese**, with joint vocals from both artists and Latin-style choreography. It was a deliberate demolition of the wall between K-pop and Latin pop.

This collaboration wasn't a musical experiment — it was **strategic groundwork**. Build a connection with Brazilian fans through "MEXE," then detonate it live in front of 2 million at Carnival. The planning was meticulous.

## JYP's Latin America Playbook — This Is Just the Beginning

When you place NMIXX's Carnival performance within JYP Entertainment's broader global strategy, the picture changes entirely.

**First, NMIXX's world tour "Episode 1: Zero Frontier" is confirmed for 2026**, covering North America, Europe, and beyond. The group has been conducting 20 fan concerts across 12 cities in Latin America, Asia, China, and Oceania — systematically building their Latin market presence.

**Second, NMIXX headlined the Viña del Mar Festival** — another historic first for K-pop at Chile's legendary music festival. Conquering both Carnival and Viña del Mar back-to-back is unprecedented.

**Third, JYP established a dedicated Latin America division in 2024** and has announced plans to debut a **Latin girl group in 2026**. NMIXX's Carnival appearance is pre-heating the market for that debut.

Here's the playbook in sequence:
1. Test the Brazilian market with "MEXE"
2. Explode Latin market awareness via Carnival + Viña del Mar
3. Complete localization with a Latin division + Latin girl group

This isn't a one-year project. It's a **three-year-plus roadmap**.

## MEARROW's Analysis — What Latin Expansion Means for K-Pop

K-pop's global expansion has historically centered on the US, Japan, and Southeast Asia. Latin America was seen as a market with passionate fans but limited monetization potential.

NMIXX's Carnival performance could flip that equation. Performing before 2 million people represents **cultural penetration that streaming numbers and album sales alone cannot measure**. The fact that K-pop echoed through Brazil's Carnival elevates the genre's standing in Latin markets to an entirely different level.

With K-pop physical album sales declining 19% in 2024 and the Korean domestic market showing its limits, Latin America could become the **next growth engine**. JYP is the first to lay the groundwork — and the head start could prove decisive.
