The most shocking tax controversy in K-pop history has erupted. ASTRO's Cha Eunwoo (real name Lee Dong-min) is under investigation by the National Tax Service for alleged tax evasion amounting to **20 billion won (approximately $13.7 million)**, with Dispatch exclusively reporting evidence that his entire family may be involved.

This bombshell hitting the top star during his military service has triggered a wave of contract cancellations from major advertisers including Shinhan Bank and Abib, sending shockwaves throughout the K-pop industry. Is this tax evasion or legitimate tax planning? And what will become of Cha Eunwoo's career?

## The Full Story Behind the 20 Billion Won Tax Assessment

The National Tax Service conducted a tax audit on Cha Eunwoo just before his military enlistment in July 2025. As a result of the investigation, the NTS issued an additional income tax assessment of **over 20 billion won**.

The core issue is that Cha Eunwoo's income was allegedly processed as **corporate income rather than personal income**. While the top personal income tax rate stands at 45%, the corporate tax rate maxes out at around 25%. The NTS determined that this difference was exploited for tax avoidance purposes.

Even more shocking is the fact that Cha Eunwoo's agency, **Fantiago, also received an additional tax assessment of 8.2 billion won**. The NTS claims that Fantiago processed fraudulent tax invoices in transactions with Cha Eunwoo's affiliated companies.

## The Paper Company Allegations: Unveiling "Cha's Gallery"

Dispatch's investigative report thoroughly exposed the reality of the corporations operated by Cha Eunwoo's family.

**Cha's Gallery**

- **Established**: 2019
- **CEO**: Cha Eunwoo (later transferred to his mother)
- **Registered Director**: Cha Eunwoo's mother
- **Financial Management**: Cha Eunwoo's father
- **Dissolved**: 2024

After Cha Eunwoo's mother took over as CEO in 2020, additional companies were established: **L&C** (artist management) and **D ANY LLC** (asset management). According to Dispatch, D ANY LLC's registered address is **the same as the eel restaurant operated by Cha Eunwoo's parents**.

The NTS claims these corporations were **"paper companies" operated solely for tax reduction purposes** without substantial business activities. If the 20 billion won tax assessment was calculated based on a 20% corporate tax rate, analysts suggest that Cha Eunwoo's taxable income could **exceed 100 billion won**.

## The Brand Exodus Domino Effect: The Harsh Reality of Advertising

The advertising industry's response was swift once the allegations surfaced.

**Brands confirmed to have withdrawn:**

- **Shinhan Bank**: Complete replacement of all advertising videos and images
- **Abib (Cosmetics)**: Removal of Cha Eunwoo from all marketing materials

Additionally, multiple other brands are reportedly suspending or reviewing their advertising campaigns featuring Cha Eunwoo. According to industry insiders, Cha Eunwoo's annual advertising income is estimated at **hundreds of billions of won**, and the losses from this incident are expected to be astronomical.

The bigger problem is that Cha Eunwoo is currently **serving in the military**. Having enlisted in July 2025, he is effectively unable to actively respond to or legally address these allegations.

## Tax Evasion vs. Tax Planning: Legal Issues and Outlook

The crux of this matter lies in distinguishing between **"tax evasion" and "tax planning."**

**Fantiago's Official Statement:**

> "The company under Cha Eunwoo's mother's name was legally established and operated as a legitimate corporation conducting actual business activities. We plan to actively respond to the NTS's judgment through the tax tribunal process."

Legal experts analyze that this case may have originated from **differing interpretations between tax authorities and the taxpayer**. If the corporations are recognized as having conducted substantial business activities, this could end as a simple civil tax dispute. However, if it is deemed intentional tax fraud, it could lead to **criminal prosecution**.

Looking at past celebrity tax controversies, most were resolved civilly after payment of additional taxes, though some cases did lead to criminal indictment.
