On May 27, 2025, Karina posted a photo to Instagram. She wore a red jacket bearing the number "2," with a rose emoji in the caption. It looked like ordinary lifestyle content — until South Korea, just a week from a presidential election, erupted in controversy.

The number 2 and the rose were symbols of the People Power Party's conservative presidential campaign.

That moment marked the beginning of a turbulent stretch that would define aespa's 2025.

## Karina: The Political Controversy That Rattled SM's Center

**What happened**

SM Entertainment and Karina's team responded quickly, calling it "everyday content with no political intent" and deleting the post. Karina herself appeared on YouTube channel *Fairy Jaehyung* and explained she was "genuinely unaware" of the political associations — she simply liked the jacket.

The public wasn't appeased. Karina's Instagram follower count dropped by **nearly half a million** from its peak. Her brand ranking plummeted. In a political climate where no celebrity wants to be perceived as partisan, Karina became tagged as a "politicized idol" — regardless of intent.

The real problem? This wasn't the only fire that year.

## Ningning: The Japan NHK Kōhaku Uta Gassen Absence

Before the dust settled on Karina's controversy, Ningning sparked her own.

Ningning posted a photo of a decorative light shaped like a mushroom. Japanese netizens immediately drew parallels to **atomic bomb mushroom clouds**, arguing the image was insensitive to victims of the Hiroshima and Nagasaki bombings.

SM Entertainment announced that Ningning would not participate in NHK's Kōhaku Uta Gassen due to influenza. But the timing — directly after the mushroom cloud controversy — fueled speculation among fans. aespa performed as a three-member group on one of Japan's most prestigious year-end stages.

Three members on the Kōhaku Uta Gassen stage became the defining image of aespa's 2025.

## Winter: The Boycott From Within the Fanbase

The third blow came not from outside, but from within their own fandom.

In November 2025, Winter's largest Chinese fanbase declared an official boycott of SM Entertainment. Their core grievance: **systematic mistreatment of Winter** by the agency — specifically, that her solo schedules were routinely absent from aespa's official channels, leaving her marginalized within the group.

The fanbase announced they would no longer purchase aespa albums, fan events, or official merchandise. This wasn't vague discontent — it was a structured, declared consumer withdrawal from one of K-pop's most financially significant overseas markets.

## Three Crises, Three Directions — Why aespa?

The three controversies have distinct characters. Karina's political scandal collided with **South Korea's domestic partisan fault lines**. Ningning's photo controversy touched the **historical wounds of East Asian geopolitics**. Winter's fandom conflict exposed **structural problems within the K-pop industry itself** — specifically, how Chinese fan markets are treated.

Three crises from three different directions converging simultaneously turned aespa into a "controversy factory" in public perception. Analysts warned of potential brand deal risks, and some outlets reported that aespa's upcoming partnerships could be affected.

But this raises the critical question: **Did any of these controversies actually impact aespa's commercial performance?**

## The Numbers Behind aespa's "Crisis"

Even as controversies swirled, aespa kept moving.

- Global tour **'Synk: Aexis Line'** ongoing (concluding at Tokyo Dome in April 2026)
- Japanese anime 'Kill Blue' opening theme **'ATTITUDE'** released (March 2026)
- **Kyocera Dome Osaka** (April 11–12), **Tokyo Dome** (April 25–26) performances booked
- Second full studio album announced for 2026 (Karina confirmed at the 2025 MAMA Awards)

Music was still being consumed. Arenas were still filling up. Their core fanbase held. So was this crisis largely a "media bubble," or a real structural threat?

## Tokyo Dome — The Real Test

On April 25, aespa takes the Tokyo Dome stage.

For a K-pop girl group, filling one of Japan's five major dome venues represents the highest commercial tier in the industry. Tokyo Dome holds approximately 55,000 people. A sold-out run would certify aespa as **an artist who survived controversy**. A weak showing would signal that the accumulated controversies had begun to erode actual consumer behavior.

## What KCL Is Watching

aespa's crisis is a textbook case of how K-pop media and fandom consume idol "mistakes."

All three controversies were **unintentional**. Karina didn't know the jacket's political connotations. Ningning's photo was a decorative light. Winter's fandom conflict stemmed from agency management, not the member herself. Yet in K-pop, **intent often matters less than outcome**.

What aespa must actually prove isn't a sold-out Tokyo Dome. It's whether their second full album — when it arrives — carries music powerful enough to transcend the noise. The group tasked with carrying SM Entertainment's next chapter is aespa. That position is too significant to concede to controversy.

K-pop history has examples of groups who came back stronger after crisis. Can aespa write themselves into that list?

## The Bottom Line

Saying "a crisis arrived" is easy. Recording how it was overcome is far harder.

aespa's 2025–2026 story isn't over. Tokyo Dome in April. A full studio album later in 2026. The next chapter has already begun.
