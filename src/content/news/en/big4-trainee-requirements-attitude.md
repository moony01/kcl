"I wasn't rejected because of my singing or dancing skills."

Every year, tens of thousands of aspiring idols knock on the doors of the Big 4 agencies (HYBE, SM, JYP, YG). Yet the final acceptance rate remains **under 0.1%**. The surprising truth? Most rejections aren't about lack of talent—they're about **attitude**. So what exactly do these industry giants look for in their trainees?

## The 5 Core Evaluation Criteria Shared by Big 4

While each agency has its own specific standards, there are five key elements that all Big 4 companies commonly assess:

**1. Potential** - They look at **growth capability** over current skill level. The key question: "Can this person shine in 3 years, even if they're lacking now?"

**2. Star Quality** - The presence that lights up in front of a camera. It's not just about looks—it's about the **aura that commands the stage**.

**3. Fundamentals** - Vocal pitch accuracy, dance rhythm, breath control—the **basic skills that form the foundation** for training.

**4. Visual** - Not necessarily the prettiest face, but rather a **camera-friendly appearance** and healthy image.

**5. Character** - And the most crucial factor. What agencies unanimously emphasize is **"attitude and character."**

## "Attitude is 90%" - Testimony from Industry Trainers

"Skills can be taught, but attitude is hard to change."

This is what a vocal trainer at a major agency tells us. The average trainee period spans **2-5 years**, with **8-12 hours daily**, **6 days a week** of intense training. While everyone's skills improve during this period, the **perseverance to never give up** and **humility to accept feedback** must come naturally.

NMIXX's Haewon revealed in an interview after passing JYP's audition:

> "The most important thing JYP looks at is **attitude**. No matter how skilled you are, you can't pass if your attitude isn't right."

This isn't unique to JYP. HYBE, SM, and YG all have separate **'Character/Attitude' sections** on their evaluation forms, and low scores here result in rejection regardless of other high marks.

## Hidden Evaluation Criteria by Agency

Beyond common standards, each Big 4 agency has evaluation criteria reflecting their **unique philosophy**.

### HYBE - "Do You Have Your Own Narrative?"

HYBE values an artist's **storytelling ability**. It's not just about singing well—you need to clearly articulate "why you want to be an idol" and "what message you want to convey." Just as BTS moved the world with 'their own story,' HYBE seeks trainees with **authentic personal narratives**.

### SM Entertainment - "Visual + Distinctive Individuality"

SM traditionally applies strong **visual standards**. But it's not just about a pretty face—they look for **uniqueness that differentiates within a group**. The key question: "What color can this person add to the group?"

### JYP Entertainment - "Character > Skill > Appearance"

This is JYP's publicly stated selection criteria by PD Park Jin-young. **"A good person"** is JYP's first requirement. Even how you treat other applicants during auditions and how you greet staff members factors into the evaluation.

### YG Entertainment - "Stage Charisma and Originality"

YG emphasizes **unique stage performance**. Rather than perfectly following choreography, they highly value the ability to add your own interpretation and **command the stage**. YG's standard: "Is this person impossible to look away from on stage?"

## 5 Types That Get Eliminated Immediately

According to casting directors, these are the types that **fail in the first round**:

**1. The Overconfident Type** - Refuses feedback, saying "But I did it well." Zero points for attitude.

**2. The Oblivious Type** - Looks at their phone while others perform or is rude to staff.

**3. The Unprepared Type** - Doesn't memorize audition song lyrics or know basic company information.

**4. Excessive Cosmetic Surgery** - Unnatural appearances that deviate from naturalness are actually deduction factors.

**5. SNS History Issues** - Past controversial social media posts. Agencies **investigate applicants' online history** before auditions.

## KCL's Perspective: Agency Rankings and Training Philosophy

Looking at KCL (K-pop Company League) agency ranking data, an interesting pattern emerges. **Agencies with systematic training programs tend to have better long-term artist performance.**

It's not about "agencies that select outstanding trainees," but rather **"agencies that turn ordinary trainees into stars"** that are truly prestigious. This comes down to the difference in systems that **select trainees with good attitudes** and **elevate their skills through systematic training**.

A common thread among agencies maintaining top positions in KCL rankings is their **high emphasis on character evaluation**. This is a strategic choice that considers **long-term artist longevity** over short-term results.

## In Closing: Practical Advice for Aspiring Trainees

If you're preparing for auditions right now, remember:

**1. You only need to show 10% of your skills** - The rest is filled by potential and attitude.

**2. Prepare your answer to "Why do you want to be an idol?"** - A sincere personal narrative moves judges.

**3. Every moment at the audition is being evaluated** - Behavior in the waiting room, attitude toward other applicants, even how you greet people.

**4. Clean up your social media** - Careless past posts can block future opportunities.

**5. Only apply through official channels** - HYBE, SM, JYP, and YG only accept applications through their official websites or emails. Beware of scams promising "guaranteed acceptance."

The journey toward becoming a K-pop idol is long and challenging. But with the **right attitude** and **relentless effort**, that dream can certainly come true. KCL is rooting for your challenge.
